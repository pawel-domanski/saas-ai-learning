
### Quick Comparison

| Feature | Supervised 🏷️ | Unsupervised 🔍 | Reinforcement 🎮 |
|---------|----------------|------------------|-------------------|
| **Data** | With labels | Without labels | Rewards/penalties |
| **Goal** | Known upfront | To discover | Maximize reward |
| **Example** | Recognizing cats | Grouping photos | Playing chess |
| **When** | You know what you want | Looking for patterns | Learning strategies |

### Examples from Different Industries

**🏥 Medicine**
- **Supervised:** Diagnosis based on symptoms (you have historical diagnoses)
- **Unsupervised:** Discovering disease subtypes (looking for unknown groups)
- **Reinforcement:** Therapy optimization (adjusting treatment to reactions)

**🛒 E-commerce**
- **Supervised:** Predicting "will buy/won't buy" (based on history)
- **Unsupervised:** Customer segmentation (discovering buyer types)
- **Reinforcement:** Dynamic pricing (testing reactions to different prices)

**🚗 Transportation**
- **Supervised:** Traffic sign recognition
- **Unsupervised:** Detecting unusual traffic patterns
- **Reinforcement:** Real-time route optimization

### When to Choose Which?

**Choose Supervised Learning when:**
- ✅ You have many examples with answers
- ✅ Problem has clear categories
- ✅ You need high accuracy
- ✅ You can define "correct" answer

**Choose Unsupervised Learning when:**
- ✅ You don't have labeled data
- ✅ Looking for hidden patterns
- ✅ Want to discover new groups
- ✅ Exploring unknown data

**Choose Reinforcement Learning when:**
- ✅ Problem requires sequence of decisions
- ✅ Environment changes
- ✅ You can define rewards
- ✅ Optimizing long-term goal

### 💡 Practical Tip

Often the best results come from **combining methods**:
1. Start with unsupervised learning to understand data
2. Use discovered groups for supervised learning
3. Apply reinforcement to optimize strategies

**Example:** Netflix recommendation system
- Unsupervised: groups similar movies
- Supervised: predicts your ratings
- Reinforcement: optimizes display order
