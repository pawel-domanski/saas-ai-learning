**Journey through AI history:**

**1960s-70s: Era of Rules**
- Attempts to encode all human knowledge into "IF...THEN..." rules
- Expert systems in medicine and law
- Problem: the world proved too complex for rules

**1980s-90s: First Steps of Learning**
- Emergence of machine learning algorithms
- Limitations: weak computers, little data
- Hybrid systems: rules + basic learning

**2010s+: Data Revolution**
- Internet provided billions of examples
- Growth in computational power (GPUs)
- Breakthrough in deep learning

**Current state:**
The best systems combine both approaches:
- **Hybrid systems**: learning with built-in safety rules
- **Reinforcement learning**: systems learn but humans correct them
- **Explainable AI**: attempts to make learning systems more transparent

**Future - where we're heading:**

**Intelligent hybrid systems:**
- Combining learning flexibility with rule reliability
- Systems switching between modes depending on situation

**Self-explaining AI:**
- Learning systems that can explain their decisions
- Solving the "black box" problem

**Personalized rule systems:**
- Rules adapting to individual needs
- Each user has their unique set of rules

**What this means for us:**
- More systems adapting to our needs
- Better control over how AI makes decisions
- More trustworthy and predictable systems

**Myths to debunk:**

❌ **Myth**: "Learning systems will replace all rule-based systems"
✅ **Fact**: Both approaches will coexist, each in their applications

❌ **Myth**: "Learning AI truly 'understands'"
✅ **Fact**: Finds statistical patterns but has no true understanding

❌ **Myth**: "Rule-based systems are obsolete"
✅ **Fact**: Still essential where transparency is needed
